1 · Density & pressure

The U-tube trick: same level, same pressure

Rule

In a connected, still liquid, every point at the same horizontal level has the same pressure.

Method

Draw a horizontal line through the lowest liquid–liquid surface. Write the pressure on the left and on the right. Set them equal.

P₀ + ρ₁gh₁ = P₀ + ρ₂gh₂

RememberAlways measure heights vertically from the common line — never along the tube.

Pascal’s law

Pressure applied to a confined fluid is transmitted undiminished to every part of the fluid and to the walls of the container.

Try it🪥 Squeeze a toothpaste tube

Pressure from your fingers reaches every point — paste comes out wherever there is an opening.

2 · Pascal’s law

The trade: force for distance

Same pressure everywhere

F₁A₁ = F₂A₂  ⇒  F₂ = F₁ × A₂A₁

The liquid is incompressible

Volume that leaves the small side = volume that enters the big side:

A₁ d₁ = A₂ d₂

RememberForce is multiplied by A₂/A₁, distance is divided by A₂/A₁. So work stays the same: F₁d₁ = F₂d₂.

"A machine has an efficiency greater than 100%. Is it possible?" (No.)

3 · Buoyancy

Where does the upthrust come from?

Step 1

The sides push equally and cancel. Only the top and bottom faces matter.

Step 2

Bottom is deeper, so it feels more pressure:
P₂ − P₁ = ρ g h  (h = height of the block)

Step 3

Net upward force = pressure difference × area:
U = ρ g h A = ρ g V

ResultUpthrust = weight of the fluid displaced.  U = V ρ g

Reveal frame by frame. The key sentence is “bottom is deeper”. hA is just the volume of the block, so ρ V g is the weight of the liquid that was pushed aside.

"Does the upthrust depend on the density of the block?" (No — only on the fluid’s density and the submerged volume.)

3 · Buoyancy

Archimedes’ principle in words

Archimedes’ principle

When a body is partly or wholly immersed in a fluid, it experiences an upward force equal to the weight of the fluid it displaces.

Where does it act?The centre of buoyancy

The centre of gravity of the displaced fluid — not of the body.

It does NOT depend onThree things

the shape, the depth (for a fully immersed body) or the body’s own density.

Three clicks. A very common error: students think a heavier body feels more upthrust. Only the displaced volume and fluid density decide it.

4 · Fluid flow

The rules of streamline flow

Rule 1

A streamline is the path of a fluid particle. The tangent at any point gives the direction of the velocity there.

Rule 2

Streamlines never cross. If they did, a particle at the crossing would have two directions at once.

Rule 3

In steady flow, the velocity at a point does not change with time — even though the particle itself moves.

Rule 4

Many streamlines together make a tube of flow. No liquid crosses its walls.

One rule per click. Rule 2 is a favourite statement in NEET assertion–reason questions.

"Can two streamlines intersect? Why not?"

8 · Capillarity

Where does h = 2T cos θ / rρg come from?

Step 1 — Upward pull of the surface

Surface tension acts along the circumference 2πr at the angle θ, so the upward force is F = 2πr T cos θ

Step 2 — Weight of the lifted column

W = (πr² h) ρ g

Step 3 — Balance

h = 2 T cos θr ρ g

Rememberh ∝ T · h ∝ 1/r · h ∝ 1/ρ · h ∝ 1/g · and r = R cos θ (R = radius of the meniscus).

Three steps. The key is that only the vertical component of surface tension supports the column. A tube of insufficient length does not make the liquid overflow: the meniscus just flattens to a smaller curvature.

"The tube is shorter than h. Will water overflow like a fountain?" (No — the meniscus radius adjusts and the liquid stays at the top.)
